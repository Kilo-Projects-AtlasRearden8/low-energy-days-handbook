# Pick One Task

## The problem with lists

To-do lists are useful on good days. On low-energy days, a long list can feel
like a wall. Every unchecked item whispers that you are falling behind. That
feeling is not motivation — it is drain.

## The practice: one task

Pick **one** task. Just one. Not three. Not "one big thing plus a few small
things." One.

It can be tiny:

- Drink a glass of water.
- Put one piece of clothing in the hamper.
- Open the laptop.
- Wash one bowl.

Tiny counts. Small is a victory. Done is done.

## How to choose

When everything feels equally important, use this order:

1. **What will make you feel better?** (drink water, move to the couch)
2. **What will take under two minutes?** (hang up one towel)
3. **What is due soonest?** (only if it truly is)

If you cannot choose, that is information too. It means today is a rest day —
and rest is a valid choice (see Rest Without Guilt).

## If you finish the one task

You are allowed to stop. You have done enough. If you genuinely want to do a
second thing, pick one more. But you are not required to.

If you do not finish the one task, that is okay too. You tried. Rest.

## ADHD-friendly tips

- Body doubling: do the task while someone else is nearby (in person or on a
  video call), even if they are doing their own thing.
- Pair the task with something pleasant: a favorite song, a show in the
  background, a warm drink.
- Use a timer for 5 or 10 minutes. You can stop when it beeps.
