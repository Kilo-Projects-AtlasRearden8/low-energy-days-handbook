# Gentle Scheduling

## The problem with rigid schedules

Hard schedules break when energy breaks. A 9:00 AM block becomes a source of
guilt when you wake up at 11:00. Rigid planning works for machines, not for
bodies with fluctuating energy.

## The practice: soft times, not deadlines

Instead of "9:00 AM — shower," try "sometime in the morning — maybe shower."
Instead of "1:00 PM — email," try "after lunch, if I have energy."

Think in blocks, not minutes:

- Morning (wake up to lunch)
- Afternoon (lunch to dinner)
- Evening (dinner to bed)

Assign at most **one or two gentle intentions** per block. Leave the rest open.

## Sample gentle day

**Morning**
- Drink water when I get up
- One small task if I feel able

**Afternoon**
- Rest or nap if needed
- One small task if energy allows

**Evening**
- Get ready for bed when ready
- No tasks after dinner (rest time)

None of these have a time attached. They are options, not obligations.

## If the day falls apart

That is expected on low-energy days. A gentle schedule bends. If you do
nothing from the schedule, the schedule still worked — it held space for you
without demanding anything.

## ADHD-friendly tips

- Use anchors instead of times: "after breakfast," "before I brush my teeth."
- Write the schedule in pencil, or on a whiteboard you can erase.
- Keep the schedule visible but not demanding: a sticky note, not a command.
