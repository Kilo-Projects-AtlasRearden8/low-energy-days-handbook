# Low-Demand Routines

## What makes a routine "low-demand"

A low-demand routine asks almost nothing of you. It has few steps, no
deadlines, and no wrong way to do it. If you skip a step, the routine is not
broken.

The goal is not efficiency. The goal is to care for yourself with the least
effort possible.

## Example routines

### Morning low-demand routine

1. Drink water
2. Brush teeth (or just rinse with water)
3. Change out of pajamas (or don't)

### Evening low-demand routine

1. Set out one thing for tomorrow (or don't)
2. Wash your face (or wipe with a cloth)
3. Get into bed when tired

### "Reset" routine (when things feel overwhelming)

1. Put on one song
2. Put five things where they belong
3. Sit down and breathe

Each step is optional. Do one. Do all. Do none.

## Build your own

Pick 2–4 steps that take under five minutes each. Write them on a routine card
(see `site/cards.html`). Keep the card where you will see it.

If a routine ever feels like a demand, simplify it. Remove steps until it feels
gentle again.

## ADHD-friendly tips

- Keep routines in the same order so they become automatic.
- Pair a routine step with something you already do ("after I pour coffee, I
  drink water").
- Use a visual reminder: the card on the mirror, the fridge, the nightstand.

## Printable cards

Routine cards are in `site/cards.html`. Print, cut out, and write your own
steps on the blank card.
